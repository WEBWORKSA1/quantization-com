Why the answers come in steps

An electron bound to a nucleus, or a particle trapped between walls, behaves as a wave that must fit its boundaries. Only certain standing-wave patterns fit, and each has a definite energy. Change level and the energy difference leaves or arrives as a single photon with E = hf = hc/λ. That is why hydrogen glows at a few sharp wavelengths instead of a smooth rainbow. Frequently asked questions

Why is the ground-state energy of hydrogen −13.6 eV?

The negative sign means the electron is bound: it takes 13.6 eV to pull it free from the ground state. Higher levels are less tightly bound, approaching 0 eV as n grows.

Which hydrogen lines are visible?

The Balmer series, which ends on level 2. Its first four lines are near 656, 486, 434 and 410 nm: red, blue-green and two violets.

How is this related to quantization in AI or audio?

Only by analogy. In computing we choose to round values onto a grid; in physics the discreteness comes from nature. Both produce a ladder of allowed values with gaps in between.
